# Europe capacity baseline — 2026-10-24

Status: complete. Europe reopened for playtesting; no test services, timers or temporary nginx routes remain. Further experiments are paused. No bot optimization is included in this baseline.

## What this measures

Europe: DigitalOcean Amsterdam, 2 vCPU, 1,967.6 MiB RAM, `DO-Premium-Intel`, Node v24.22.1. The generator is a separate Apple M3 Max machine (14 cores, 36 GiB, Node v24.11.1). Production Brawlock services stayed installed and idle; the tested region was drained, with the other region available.

The frozen game source is `a25274d9069d8e53b5ca65023ab88e32bbe20f83`, netcode `d3d1f10fe9d4`: the deployed alpha.10 runtime plus its operations-only verification follow-up. It was exported from Git, not taken from the changing working tree. Each subsequent run copies its server/client bundles before starting. Concurrent bot edits cannot change a running benchmark.

Synthetic rooms run in a temporary nonroot service, separate SQLite databases and a 384 MiB V8 heap/512 MiB cgroup. No production accounts, Elo, rewards or GA events are used. Production account-service capacity is **not** measured.

Final network results use independent **direct WSS connections through nginx/TLS** on an IP-restricted temporary path. SSH is control-plane only, with no port forwarding. The route is removed and production admissions reopened afterward, also by an independent safety timer if the operator exits unexpectedly.

The earlier SSH-tunneled pilot is **excluded from the capacity conclusion**. It developed latency/rate-limit failures at 60 clients that did not reproduce in the direct 60-client stage. This does not prove a particular network fault, but it is sufficient reason not to mix its latency data into the baseline.

## Human-heavy result

Ten independent real session/prediction clients per room, zero server bots. They move, aim, cast, shop, receive acknowledgements and verify authoritative stream hashes. Time scale 1×; ten-second warmup then a 75-second observation per stage. Shop time is shortened to eight seconds; economy is the deployed quick-play 50/15/2/3 preset. Worlds/seeds are not paired across network stages.

CPU below is **process CPU: one full core = 100%**, not percentage of the two-core host. RTT and input acknowledgement columns are p95 across sampled client measurements; RTT values are smoothed transport measurements, not raw packet percentiles.

| Rooms | Active clients | CPU median / p95 | RSS max | Hz/room median | Client RTT p95 | Input ack p95 | Observation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 4 | 40 | 40.8 / 49.3% | 107 MiB | 60.0 | 76 ms | 138 ms | Healthy |
| 6 | 60 | 60.5 / 77.3% | 109 MiB | 60.0 | 83 ms | 152 ms | Healthy |
| 8 | 80 | 84.5 / 94.2% | 115 MiB | 60.0 | 99 ms | 177 ms | Last healthy tested stage, little CPU headroom |
| 10 | 100 | 91.4 / 103.3% | 121 MiB | 60.0 | 5,485 ms | 8,246 ms | Unacceptable input/transport backlog |
| 12 | 120 | 95.2 / 105.4% | 129 MiB | 60.0 | 7,481 ms | 13,508 ms | Operator interrupted the ramp; not a passing stage |

No stream desyncs occurred. A 60 Hz simulation counter alone was insufficient: interaction was already unusable at 100 clients. The initial ceiling runner did not yet stop on input latency; the operator stopped it and the tool now stops after five samples with input-ack p95 above 500 ms.

The idle production health endpoint's p95 was 108–118 ms at 60–80 clients, 208 ms at 100 and 247 ms at 120. Some common-path/network or shared-host overhead also rose, but it was far smaller than the multi-second game-channel delays. These observations identify an end-to-end operating boundary for this generator and deployment; they do not isolate the exact queue or certify a universal hardware limit. The generator's own event loops remained responsive, with zero desyncs. Early `generatorCpuSumP95` used an overlapping sample window and can double-count adjacent worker samples; do not use that field as an exact generator-utilization measurement for this run. Later runs use one nearest sample per worker.

A provisional human-heavy budget is **six full rooms / 60 active people**, leaving headroom below the observed eight-room boundary. This is a short-run planning number, not an admission cap or a service guarantee. Confirm with boundary repeats, later-round workloads and a longer soak before relying on it commercially. RAM was not the binding resource in these stages.

Raw direct human data: `artifacts/capacity/eu-1792863175072/`, including `results/{manifest.json,results.json,samples.jsonl}`, saved bundles and the actual runner used. In this first direct run, `monitorRttMs` measured the combined wait for monitor and control requests; the independent `controlRttMs` is valid. ## AI component cost

Offline profile on the same Europe host: three fixed seeds per difficulty, ten combatants, 300 simulated seconds per seed after warmup. Work is normalized to 60 ticks/second; the loop runs faster than real time to measure component cost. Every case purchased equipment, moved, cast and advanced rounds. These numbers time **only `think()`**, separately from core `step()`. They normalize elapsed component time to an equivalent one-core budget; they are not separate kernel CPU counters and can include scheduling/GC pauses.

| Difficulty | One bot's AI, percentage points of one core (three-seed range) | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Medium | 0.007–0.013% | All phases, including dead/shop intervals |
| Hard | 0.024–0.031% | About 0.24–0.31 ms of AI work per real second at 60 Hz |
| Brutal | 0.024–0.025% | Similar order of cost to Hard in these traces |

Alive-fight AI cost was about 0.026–0.033% per Hard/Brutal bot. Nine such AI brains alone are roughly 0.22–0.28% of one core in these traces. **This is not the full cost of nine bot seats.** It excludes their world/projectile effects, cloning/hashing, scheduled input plans, serialization, transport, timers, account work and rendering. Do not subtract this offline profile from a different networked world and call the remainder a precisely measured subsystem cost.

Raw component profile: `artifacts/capacity/eu-1792863760395/bot-cost.json`. Same netcode/source baseline; fixed seeds 101/202/303. No local laptop timings are used as Europe timings.

## Bot-heavy capacity and roster comparison

The capacity ramp fixes ten combatants per room: one real client plus nine server bots (seven Hard, one Medium, one Brutal). Direct WSS/TLS, ten-second warmup and 75-second observations match the human-heavy test. All stages had zero desyncs.

| Rooms / humans | Bots | CPU median / p95 | RSS max | Hz/room p05 | RTT p95 / input ack p95 | Observation |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 4 | 36 | 26.7 / 36.8% | 103 MiB | 59.75 | 62 / 112 ms | Healthy |
| 8 | 72 | 52.5 / 62.7% | 110 MiB | 59.77 | 64 / 118 ms | Healthy |
| 12 | 108 | 74.1 / 84.3% | 117 MiB | 59.80 | 65 / 108 ms | Healthy, proposed planning point |
| 16 | 144 | 96.7 / 105.4% | 128 MiB | 59.78 | 71 / 126 ms | Responsive, little CPU headroom |
| 24 | 216 | 124.0 / 129.0% | 150 MiB | 58.49 | 96 / 214 ms | Larger stalls; worst one-second loop p95 175 ms |
| 32 | 288 | 121.5 / 128.0% | 155 MiB | 50.47 | 167 / 480 ms | Stopped early for sustained simulation throughput loss |

At 32 rooms some clients received only about 44–52 confirmed ticks/second in their p05 windows, while generator event-loop maxima remained below 43 ms. The final stage stopped after about 25 seconds of measured load (38 seconds including room cleanup). The lower median CPU at 32 is not extra headroom: delivery had already degraded.

A provisional sparse-room budget is **12 simultaneously active humans in 12 separate bot-filled games**. Six full human games supported 60 people with lower median process CPU in the separate run. Packing humans into existing rooms matters substantially to cost per human. Do not combine the two extreme budgets or describe the 144 bots at the edge as 144 additional human players.

Raw direct bot-heavy data: `artifacts/capacity/eu-1792863793657/`. All synthetic services and temporary nginx routes were cleaned up after this ramp.

The roster comparison fixed four rooms and one real client per room for 120 seconds per roster, with CPU profiling enabled in all three cases. All remained at about 60 Hz with zero desyncs. These are profiled workload medians, not exact unprofiled room costs.

| Bots per room | Process CPU median | Equivalent CPU per room | Downlink payload per human |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 20.5% | 5.11% | 6.8 KiB/s |
| 4 | 23.9% | 5.98% | 12.1 KiB/s |
| 9 | 30.3% | 7.58% | 23.7 KiB/s |

The observed increment was roughly **0.29–0.32 percentage points of one core per added bot** across these roster sizes, including world simulation/replication and transport effects. Pure decision work was only around 0.02–0.03 points. Reducing nine bots to four cut the four-room process median from 30.3% to 23.9% (about 21%) in this run; it did not remove the substantial fixed room cost. These are directional estimates: worlds/outcomes differ, the profiler adds overhead, and the last roster saw bursts of hypervisor steal time. Repeat before changing defaults solely for cost. Payload counters exclude WebSocket/TLS/TCP overhead, media and accounts.

The 1 ms CPU profile attributed non-idle samples approximately as follows: 31% native/unmapped, 14% garbage collection, 13% transport/stream/wire, 9% session/scheduling, 8% server/persistence/monitoring, 8% other JavaScript, 7% cloning/hashing, 5% bot decisions and 4% core simulation. These are exclusive sample counts attributed to the nearest mapped JavaScript caller, not shares of the kernel process CPU counter. They include setup, all roster sizes and cleanup. The profile contains one −3 µs timestamp delta; count-based attribution retains every valid sample and does not infer elapsed time from it.

## Retention and verification

Ignored raw measurements, frozen artifacts and diagnostic profiles are retained under `artifacts/capacity/` for at least 30 days, with no automatic deletion. Private token/config files must not be published. Synthetic services are stopped and temporary routes removed between experiments; databases and receipts remain for review.

Separately, the operations dashboard now supports 30 days of history. A three-service/10-second synthetic month contains 777,600 raw samples and occupied about 158 MiB including indexes and hourly summaries. The month query dropped from 1.37 seconds scanning raw samples to about 6 ms using summaries on the local M3 machine; startup backfill took about 1.6 seconds once. A record/fold measured about 0.6 ms locally. These are storage/query checks, not Europe timings. Live data already pruned by the old seven-day policy cannot be reconstructed.

The associated match-feed/admin changes passed 1,771 Vitest tests, nine monitoring Node tests, typecheck, client/server/account builds and desktop fixture checks at 1440×900 and 1280×720. They are prepared locally; this capacity exercise does not deploy those application changes.

## Remaining before a pricing decision

Measure a representative mix of full shared rooms and sparse bot-filled rooms, longer matches with varied upgraded builds, completion/reconnect bursts, ranked/accounts/presence and authority persistence, multiple seeds/repeats and a one-to-two-hour soak. Repeat the boundary with a second independent generator to separate any remaining WAN/NIC limits.

Use actual invoices and transfer allowances for currency figures. Report payload bytes per active player-second separately from TLS/TCP overhead, static/media downloads and account traffic. Divide allocated fleet cost by actual occupied player-hours, not configured slots or the highest short-run count. No monthly price, overage allowance or purchase break-even is assumed here.
