# Brawlock — 60-second teaser music, rounds 2 and 3

A fresh Suno prompt round for a roughly **60-second teaser edit**, with **90 seconds as the upper bound for this audition round**. Instrumental dark-fantasy game music: ritual percussion, low strings, iron/bronze and threatening brass. The main theme should be recognizable before the montage is busy.

The previous trailer brief aimed for 75–90 seconds. This round establishes its identity earlier, builds quickly and gives the title a written ending. No audio has been generated as part of this document.

Start with **D — Straight Into the Arena** for the current brief: a two-second identity hook, battle rhythm by three to four seconds, and active fighting through most of the teaser. ## Intended 60-second edit

This is the revised edit rhythm for D–F, not a locked shot list. F uses a 120 BPM pulse: an edit beat every half-second and a four-beat bar every two seconds. D and E run faster; cut to their actual phrases instead of forcing identical frame timings.

| Time | Music | Picture / sound opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 s maximum | One bell/iron accent and a short memorable hook | One quick arena or warlock reveal; cut directly toward the first exchange |
| 4–12 s | Battle rhythm already established | Show a real fight: movement, the first spell and a counter or impact |
| 12–28 s | Add brass answers and more rhythmic weight | Spell variety, dodges and knockbacks; clear action before the montage speeds up |
| 28–38 s | Raise urgency and percussion density | Escalating exchanges and arena/lava danger; selected SFX remain audible |
| Around 38–40 s | A beat or brief pocket for a featured impact | One readable finishing hit or counterplay moment; return to action immediately |
| 40–56 s | Strongest battle statement and final cadence | Best combat clips and final clash |
| 56–60 s | Title accent, then natural decay | BRAWLOCK title and short call to action |

Tempo, timestamps and ending length in Styles or Lyrics are requests, not sample-accurate controls. Audition the actual recording; conform the winner to the finished 60-second video edit. Prefer a complete musical arc near one minute. A promising take up to 90 seconds can be edited down if the hook, transitions and ending work. If it develops into a long song with a late payoff, reject it for this round rather than extending it.

## Audition this batch

1. For this new batch, generate one pair each of D, E and F: six candidates total. Start with D's pair if you want to audition one direction first.
2. Judge the first four seconds first: is the identity clear, and has the battle rhythm arrived? Reject a take that stays in a long ambient introduction.
3. Check that the music supports a real fight by four seconds, then escalates further instead of reaching its maximum immediately. Keep a brief SFX pocket and an obvious final title hit. Judge the actual music, not whether it followed the written timestamps literally.
4. Try the promising takes under footage with the real spell and knockout sounds. A cue that overwhelms every hit is a weak trailer mix even when it sounds impressive alone.
5. Refine the winning direction before generating more styles. Keep its reference and musical hook stable.
